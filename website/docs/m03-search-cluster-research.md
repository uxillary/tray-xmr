# M03 search cluster research

Research checked 6 October 2026. Search intent below is qualitative; no search-volume figures are available or implied.

## Search landscape

Representative searches cluster around a visible XMRig status or a practical question: “XMRig huge pages 0% Windows”, “XMRig huge pages permission granted but not working”, “failed to apply MSR mod hashrate will be low”, “why is XMRig hashrate low”, “XMRig not using all CPU threads”, and “how much RAM does RandomX need”. Search results included years-old community posts with contradictory instructions, alongside XMRig's maintained technical reference. The recurring editorial opportunity is to explain what each startup indicator does and does not prove, give a safe order of checks, and link related concepts without promising a target hashrate.

XMRig's docs directly cover Huge Pages and the Windows `SeLockMemoryPrivilege`, RandomX's memory/cache requirements, MSR presets, CPU configuration and benchmarking. The documentation is authoritative and technical; the pages below add a Windows reader's diagnostic framing, definitions, cautions and cross-links. Search results also surfaced a third-party XMRig troubleshooting site with similar topic targeting; it is not used as a technical authority. Ember's differentiation should therefore come from accuracy, primary sourcing, concise diagnostics and restraint, not from a larger page count.

## Internal-link model

Troubleshooting hub surfaces the Huge Pages, MSR and low-hashrate pages first. Learn hub surfaces the thread and memory/cache explainers first. Huge Pages links to cache and low hashrate; MSR links to low hashrate and Huge Pages; low hashrate links to all relevant diagnostics and both learning pages; each learning page links to its related concept and symptom guides. Every article includes a breadcrumb, a return-to-hub link and visible source links.

## Sources and method

Technical claims were checked against the linked XMRig primary documentation and Microsoft Learn on 6 October 2026. Representative queries and competitor observations are qualitative readings of public search results on that date; they are not volume estimates or claims about ranking. Community results helped identify wording only, not establish technical facts. Search can vary by location, date and personalization.
